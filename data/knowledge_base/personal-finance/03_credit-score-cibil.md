## What Is a Credit Score?

A credit score is a 3-digit number between 300 and 900 that summarises your
creditworthiness — the likelihood that you will repay a loan. Lenders check it
before granting loans, credit cards, or even setting interest rates.

In India, **TransUnion CIBIL** is the most widely used credit bureau. Others
include Equifax, Experian, and CRIF High Mark — collectively called Credit
Information Companies (CICs). Banks report to all bureaus monthly.

---

## The Score Range

| Score range | Category | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| 750–900 | Excellent | Easy loan approvals, lowest interest rates |
| 700–749 | Good | Approvals likely; competitive rates |
| 650–699 | Fair | Approvals possible; higher interest rates |
| 600–649 | Poor | Difficult approvals; may need collateral |
| 300–599 | Very poor | Likely rejection; major repair needed |

**Target: 750 or above.** Most banks use 750 as the threshold for a standard
home loan approval; some premium products require 800+.

---

## What Builds (and Damages) Your Score

### Factors that determine your CIBIL score:

| Factor | Weight | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Payment history | ~35% | On-time payments boost; late payments/defaults destroy |
| Credit utilisation | ~30% | % of credit limit used; keep below 30% |
| Credit age | ~15% | Older accounts boost the score |
| Credit mix | ~10% | Mix of secured (home/car loan) and unsecured (credit card) is positive |
| Recent enquiries | ~10% | Multiple new loan applications in short time lowers score |

---

### Factors that hurt your score:

- **Missed or late EMI/credit card payments** — single most damaging factor
- **Settling a loan for less than owed** — marked as "Settled", not "Closed"
- **High credit utilisation** — using ₹90,000 of a ₹1 lakh card limit is risky
- **Too many loan applications in a short period** — each hard enquiry lowers
  score temporarily
- **Guaranteeing someone else's loan** — their default affects your score
- **Not having any credit history** — a score of "NH" (No History) can be as
  difficult as a low score

## How to Build or Improve Your Score

### Starting from no credit history:

1. **Get a secured credit card** — give a fixed deposit as collateral; the card
   limit equals the FD amount; use it for small purchases and pay in full monthly
2. **Consumer durable loan** — 0% EMI purchase creates a loan record
3. **Credit-builder loan** — some NBFCs offer these specifically for newcomers

### Improving an existing score:

1. **Never miss a payment** — set up auto-pay for the minimum due; pay the full
   amount whenever possible
2. **Reduce credit utilisation below 30%** — if your limit is ₹1 lakh, keep
   outstanding below ₹30,000. Request a limit increase to reduce utilisation
   ratio without changing spend
3. **Don't close old credit cards** — older credit age helps the score; a card
   with zero balance and no annual fee costs nothing to keep
4. **Avoid applying for multiple loans simultaneously** — space applications by
   at least 6 months
5. **Monitor your report** — errors (incorrect defaults, accounts you didn't
   open) are common; dispute them promptly

## Checking Your CIBIL Report

You can get one free credit report per year from each bureau:

- **CIBIL:** mycibil.com (TransUnion)
- **Equifax:** equifax.co.in
- **Experian:** experian.in
- **CRIF:** crifhighmark.com

Monthly monitoring apps: BankBazaar, PaisaBazaar, OneScore — these do a "soft
enquiry" that does not affect your score.

**What to check on your report:**
- Personal information accuracy (name, DOB, PAN)
- Account list — ensure no unfamiliar accounts
- Payment history — verify no incorrect late payments
- Enquiry list — check for applications you didn't make

---

## Disputing Errors

If you find incorrect information:
1. File a dispute on the bureau's website (CIBIL has an online dispute form)
2. Write to the lender that reported the error — they must correct it within 30
   days per RBI guidelines
3. Keep copies of all communication

Errors take 30–45 days to resolve. Disputes don't hurt your score during review.

## Credit Score and Loan Interest Rates

Your score directly affects the cost of borrowing:

| Home loan amount: ₹50 lakh, 20 years | CIBIL 750+ | CIBIL 650–700 |
|---|---|---|
| Approximate interest rate | 8.5% | 10.5% |
| Monthly EMI | ₹43,391 | ₹49,919 |
| Total interest paid | ₹54.1 lakh | ₹69.8 lakh |
| **Extra cost for lower score** | — | **₹15.7 lakh more** |

A good credit score saves lakhs over a lifetime of borrowing.

---

## What Does NOT Affect Your Score

- Checking your own credit report (soft enquiry)
- Debit card usage
- Bank savings account balance
- Salary or income level (not reported to bureaus)
- Investment portfolio

---

## Summary

- Target a CIBIL score of 750+ for the best loan rates.
- Pay EMIs and credit card bills on time, every time — this is the single
  biggest factor.
- Keep credit card utilisation below 30% of your limit.
- Don't apply for multiple loans simultaneously.
- Check your free credit report annually for errors; dispute them promptly.
