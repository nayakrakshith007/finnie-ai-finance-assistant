## What it is for

An emergency fund is money set aside to cover living expenses when income stops or an unplanned
cost lands — job loss, a medical event not covered by insurance, an urgent family expense. Its
purpose is not growth. Its purpose is to stop you from selling long-term investments at a bad
time or borrowing at credit-card rates, which in India run around 36% to 42% a year.

This is why it is usually treated as step one, before any investing: without it, every
investment doubles as an emergency fund, and gets liquidated at the worst moment.

## How much

The size is based on **expenses, not income** — rent, EMIs, groceries, utilities, school fees,
insurance premiums, transport.

- **Three months** for a stable government or large-company job, dual-income household, no
  dependants.
- **Six months** for most salaried people with dependants.
- **Nine to twelve months** for a single earner supporting a family, anyone with variable income
  (freelance, commission, business), or someone in a sector with long job-search cycles.

A household spending ₹50,000 a month would therefore target ₹1.5 lakh to ₹6 lakh, depending on
circumstances.

## Where to keep it

The test is accessibility within a day or two, with no risk of being down when needed. That
rules out equity entirely.

Common structure: one month's expenses in a savings account for instant access, the rest split
between a **sweep-in fixed deposit** (auto-liquidates as needed, earns FD rates) and a
**liquid or overnight mutual fund** (redemption typically T+1; some offer instant redemption up
to ₹50,000). Liquid funds are not guaranteed, but overnight and liquid categories hold very
short-maturity paper and are the least volatile debt categories.

## Building it without derailing everything else

Three mechanics make this work: a standing instruction the day after salary credit so it is not
discretionary, routing windfalls like bonuses or tax refunds into it until the target is met, and
keeping it in a separate account from daily spending so it is not quietly consumed.

Replenishing it after use takes priority over resuming investments — otherwise the next emergency
finds you unprotected.

*Educational information only. Interest rates and redemption timelines vary by product and
