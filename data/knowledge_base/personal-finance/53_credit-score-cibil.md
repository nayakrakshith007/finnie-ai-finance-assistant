## The four bureaus

India has four RBI-licensed credit information companies: TransUnion CIBIL, Experian, Equifax,
and CRIF High Mark. "CIBIL score" is used colloquially for all of them, but each maintains its
own file, and scores differ because lenders do not report to all four identically.

CIBIL scores run 300 to 900. Roughly, 750 and above is treated as strong, 700 to 749 as
acceptable, and below 650 as likely to attract rejection or a higher interest rate. A new
borrower with no credit history shows -1 or NA, which is not a bad score but an absent one.

## What moves the score

- **Repayment history (~30–35%)** — the largest factor. Even one payment 30 days late is
  reported and visible for years.
- **Credit utilisation (~25–30%)** — balance used as a share of total card limit. Staying under
  30% is the commonly cited threshold; a maxed-out card hurts even when paid in full monthly.
- **Age of credit (~15%)** — longer histories score better, which is why closing your oldest card
  can lower the score.
- **Credit mix (~10%)** — a blend of secured loans (home, auto) and unsecured (card, personal)
  reads better than unsecured alone.
- **Recent enquiries (~10%)** — each loan application triggers a hard enquiry. Several in a short
  window reads as credit-hungry behaviour.

Checking your own score is a **soft enquiry** and does not affect it. RBI requires every bureau
to provide one free full credit report per year.

## Why it matters beyond approval

The score sets the price, not just the yes or no. On a ₹50 lakh home loan over 20 years, a
0.5 percentage point difference in rate changes total interest by several lakh rupees. Insurers
and some employers also reference credit history.

## Fixing a damaged score

There is no quick repair, and any service promising one is worth avoiding. What works: clear
overdue amounts first, since a live default outweighs everything else; set auto-debit for at
least the minimum due so nothing is missed; bring utilisation down by paying mid-cycle or
requesting a limit increase; and dispute genuine errors directly with the bureau, which must
investigate within 30 days under RBI rules.

Score recovery after clearing defaults typically takes 6 to 18 months of clean behaviour.
