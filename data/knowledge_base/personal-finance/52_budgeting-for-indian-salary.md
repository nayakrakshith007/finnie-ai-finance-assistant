## Start from take-home, not CTC

Indian salary structures make this step necessary. The CTC figure in an offer letter includes the
employer's PF contribution, gratuity provisioning, and sometimes insurance premiums — none of
which reach your bank account. Budget from **net monthly credit**, after income tax, the
employee PF deduction, and professional tax.

A ₹12 lakh CTC frequently lands as roughly ₹80,000 to ₹85,000 in hand, not ₹1 lakh.

## The framework

50-30-20 splits take-home pay three ways:

- **50% needs** — rent, utilities, groceries, transport, EMIs, insurance premiums, school fees.
- **30% wants** — eating out, subscriptions, travel, gadgets, entertainment.
- **20% savings and investments** — emergency fund first, then goal-linked investing.

Its value is as a starting diagnostic, not a law. If needs consume 70%, the framework has told
you something useful about fixed costs.

## Where it breaks in Indian conditions

**Metro rent.** In Mumbai, Bengaluru, or Delhi NCR, rent alone can be 30% to 40% of take-home,
pushing needs well past 50%. The usual adaptation is treating 50-30-20 as a direction of travel
while capping rent nearer 25% to 30% where the city allows it.

**EPF already counts.** Your 12% employee PF contribution is saving. Someone saving 8% in mutual
funds plus 12% in EPF has already hit 20%.

**Family obligations.** Support for parents or siblings is a fixed need in many Indian
households, not a discretionary want, and budget frameworks built elsewhere do not account for
it.

**Irregular large costs.** Festival spending, annual insurance premiums, and weddings are not
monthly but are not optional either. Dividing the annual total by twelve and setting it aside
monthly ("sinking funds") prevents these from becoming credit-card balances.

## The number that actually matters

The savings rate — what share of take-home pay you invest — drives outcomes far more than fund
selection in the early years. Increasing it from 10% to 20% roughly halves the time to any given
corpus. Directing a fixed share of every raise to investments, before lifestyle absorbs it, is
the mechanism that makes the rate rise over a career.

*Educational information only. Salary structures and tax deductions vary by employer and
regime.*
