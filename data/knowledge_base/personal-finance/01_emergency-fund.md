## What Is an Emergency Fund?

An emergency fund is a dedicated pool of money set aside exclusively for
genuine financial emergencies — job loss, sudden medical expenses, urgent
home or vehicle repairs, or any unexpected event that disrupts your income
or creates large unplanned costs.

It is not invested in the market. It is not for planned expenses. It is a
liquid, readily accessible buffer that keeps you from going into debt or
selling long-term investments at the wrong time when life throws a curveball.

---

## Why You Need One Before Anything Else

The emergency fund is the first step in personal finance — before investing,
before paying off low-interest debt aggressively, before saving for goals.

Without one:
- A job loss forces you to liquidate SIPs or FDs at inopportune times
- A medical emergency puts you in debt at high interest rates (personal loans,
  credit card at 36–42% per year)
- Market crashes feel catastrophic because the portfolio is also your safety net

With one, you can stay invested during downturns without being forced to sell.

---

## How Much Is Enough?

**Standard rule:** 3–6 months of essential monthly expenses.

Essential expenses include: rent/EMI, groceries, utilities, insurance premiums,
school fees, loan EMIs, and basic transport. It does NOT include dining out,
subscriptions, vacations, or discretionary spending.

**Adjust based on your situation:**

| Situation | Recommended fund size |
|---|---|
| Salaried, stable industry, dual income | 3 months |
| Salaried, single income, dependants | 6 months |
| Self-employed, freelancer, variable income | 9–12 months |
| Nearing retirement | 12–24 months |

**Example calculation:**
- Monthly rent: ₹20,000
- Groceries + utilities: ₹15,000
- Insurance: ₹3,000
- School fees (monthly): ₹7,000
- Loan EMI: ₹25,000
- **Total essential expenses: ₹70,000/month**
- **6-month emergency fund target: ₹4.2 lakh**

---

## Where to Keep It

The emergency fund must be:
1. **Liquid** — accessible within 24–48 hours
2. **Safe** — no market risk; principal must not fall
3. **Separated** — not in your regular salary account (reduces temptation)

**Best options:**

| Option | Return | Liquidity | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| High-interest savings account | 4–7% | Instant | Easy to open; no lock-in |
| Liquid mutual fund | 6–7% | 1–2 days | Slight delay; still good |
| Overnight fund | 6–7% | Next day | Very safe; low duration risk |
| Sweep FD (auto-sweep) | 6–7% | Instant | Bank converts excess savings to FD |

**Avoid:** Regular FDs with penalty for premature withdrawal, equity funds,
stocks, or crypto for emergency funds — these can drop in value exactly when
you need the money most.

---

## How to Build It

If you don't have an emergency fund yet, build it systematically:

1. **Start a dedicated savings account** separate from your salary account
2. **Automate a monthly transfer** right after salary credit — treat it like a
   mandatory expense, not optional saving
3. **Target ₹10,000–20,000/month** until you reach your goal
4. **Any windfalls** (bonus, tax refund, gift money) → go straight to the
   emergency fund until it's full
5. **Don't invest until it's funded** — this is the prerequisite

---

## When to Use It (and When Not To)

**Use it for:**
- Unexpected medical bills not covered by insurance
- Job loss (covering expenses during job search)
- Urgent home repair (burst pipe, electrical fault)
- Urgent vehicle repair if vehicle is needed for work

**Do NOT use it for:**
- Planned expenses you forgot to budget for (vacations, festivals, weddings)
- Investment "opportunities" — it is not a source of investment funds
- Consumer purchases (electronics, clothes)
- Paying down debt unless it is a true emergency

---

## After Using It

If you dip into your emergency fund, rebuilding it is the top priority before
resuming other financial goals. Resume the same monthly transfer until the fund
is restored.

---

## Summary

- Build an emergency fund of 3–6 months of essential expenses before investing.
- Keep it in a high-interest savings account or liquid fund — not in the market.
- Automate contributions monthly; treat it as a non-negotiable expense.
- Use only for genuine emergencies; rebuild immediately after using it.
