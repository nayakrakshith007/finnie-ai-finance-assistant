## Why Budgeting Matters

Most people who struggle financially don't have an income problem — they have
a cash-flow awareness problem. Without tracking where money goes, lifestyle
inflation silently absorbs every raise, bonus, and income increase.

A budget is not a restriction. It is a plan that tells your money where to go
instead of wondering where it went.

---

## The 50-30-20 Rule

The 50-30-20 rule is a simple, flexible budgeting framework:

| Category | Allocation | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| Needs | 50% of take-home pay | Rent/EMI, groceries, utilities, insurance, transport, minimum loan payments |
| Wants | 30% of take-home pay | Dining out, entertainment, subscriptions, hobbies, vacations |
| Savings & Investments | 20% of take-home pay | Emergency fund, SIPs, PPF, NPS, debt repayment above minimum |

**Example for ₹1 lakh monthly take-home salary:**
- Needs: ₹50,000 (rent ₹20,000, groceries ₹10,000, EMI ₹15,000, utilities + insurance ₹5,000)
- Wants: ₹30,000 (dining out ₹8,000, subscriptions ₹2,000, shopping ₹10,000, leisure ₹10,000)
- Savings: ₹20,000 (SIP ₹10,000, PPF ₹5,000, emergency fund top-up ₹5,000)

---

## Adapting the Rule to India

The 50-30-20 rule was designed for Western income levels. In India, the
ratios may need adjustment:

**High cost-of-living cities (Mumbai, Delhi, Bengaluru):**
- Needs often consume 55–60% of take-home for middle-income earners
- Adjust: Needs 60%, Wants 20%, Savings 20%

**Aggressive savings phase (early career, high income):**
- Target: Needs 40%, Wants 20%, Savings 40%
- This accelerates wealth-building significantly

**Rule of thumb:** Never let savings fall below 10% of take-home; aim for 20%+.

## The "Pay Yourself First" Principle

The most effective savings habit: automate your savings before spending begins.

1. Set up an auto-transfer on salary credit day — SIP debits, PPF transfer,
   emergency fund top-up all happen automatically
2. Whatever remains is available to spend
3. You never miss money you never see

This is more powerful than "save what's left at month-end" — that approach
typically results in saving ₹0 as spending expands to fill income.

## Tracking Your Budget

You cannot improve what you don't measure. Tracking methods:

**Simple method — zero-based budget:**
Assign every rupee a purpose at the start of the month:
- Categorise expected expenses
- Allocate the full take-home across categories
- Track actual vs budget weekly

**Apps:** Money Manager, Walnut, ETMONEY, Splitwise (for shared expenses)

**Spreadsheet:** A simple Google Sheet with income, fixed expenses, and
variable spending by category works for most people

**Bank statement review:** Monthly review of bank + credit card statements;
tag each transaction as Need/Want/Saving

## Common Budgeting Mistakes

**1. Budgeting for income, not take-home**
Budget on actual salary credited to bank (after PF, TDS), not gross CTC.

**2. Forgetting irregular expenses**
Annual insurance premiums, car servicing, festivals, birthday gifts. Divide
by 12 and budget monthly ("sinking fund" method).

**3. Not accounting for EMI as a fixed need**
EMIs are non-negotiable needs, not savings. Count them in the 50% bucket.

**4. Treating credit card spend as "not real money"**
Credit card spend is real debt. Budget the same whether paid by cash, UPI,
or credit card.

**5. Setting an unrealistically tight budget**
A budget with no wants allocation will fail within weeks. Build in discretionary
spending guilt-free.

## Lifestyle Inflation — The Silent Wealth Killer

Every time income rises, resist the automatic urge to upgrade everything.

- Salary goes up ₹20,000 → entire raise absorbed in higher rent, new gadget,
  more dining out → savings unchanged
- **Alternative:** When income rises, increase savings/investments first, then
  allow selective lifestyle upgrades

A ₹5,000/month SIP started at 25 invested at 12% per year grows to:
- 10 years: ₹11.6 lakh
- 20 years: ₹49.9 lakh
- 30 years: ₹1.76 crore

Each ₹5,000/month consumed as lifestyle expenses instead = ₹0 at retirement.

## The Budget Review Cycle

**Monthly:** Review actual vs planned for the previous month. Note overspend
categories.

**Quarterly:** Assess whether the savings rate is on track; adjust category
allocations.

**Annual:** Full review — update for income changes, life events (marriage,
child, new loan), insurance renewals, goal progress.

---

## Summary

- Use 50-30-20 as a starting framework: needs, wants, savings.
- In India, needs often run 55–60% — adjust wants downward, not savings.
- Automate savings at the start of the month; spend what remains.
- Track spending monthly; review against budget.
- Guard against lifestyle inflation — increase savings when income rises.
