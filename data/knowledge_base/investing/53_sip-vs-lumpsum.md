## The question is usually mis-framed

Most people do not actually choose between SIP and lump sum. If you invest from monthly salary,
a SIP is simply what matching investment to cash flow looks like. The genuine choice arises only
when a large sum arrives at once — a bonus, a maturity, an inheritance, property sale proceeds.

## What the arithmetic says

Markets rise more often than they fall over long periods. So investing a lump sum immediately
puts more money to work earlier and, on average across historical periods, finishes ahead of
staggering the same amount in.

But "on average" hides the distribution. Deploying a lump sum just before a sharp drawdown
produces a painful early loss on the entire amount, and the behavioural consequence — abandoning
the plan near the bottom — tends to be more damaging than the drawdown itself.

So the two framings answer different questions:

- *Which maximises expected return?* Lump sum, usually.
- *Which limits regret and the chance of abandoning the plan?* Staggering.

## Horizon matters more than either

Equity exposure for money needed within three years carries real risk of being down when you
need it, whichever way it was invested. The staggering-versus-lump-sum debate is second-order
compared with matching asset class to time horizon.

## STP: the middle path

A Systematic Transfer Plan parks the lump sum in a liquid or arbitrage fund and transfers a
fixed amount into the target equity scheme at set intervals. The waiting money earns something
rather than sitting idle, and entry gets spread over months.

Two mechanics worth knowing: each transfer out of the source fund is a **redemption**, so it is
a taxable event on those gains, and the transfer happens on fixed dates regardless of market
level — an STP is not a tactical tool.

A common structure is spreading a large sum over six to twelve months, which is less about
optimality than about making it psychologically possible to stay invested.

*Educational information only. This describes trade-offs between approaches, not a recommendation
of either for any individual.*
