## One scheme, two price tags

Every mutual fund scheme in India offers two plans holding exactly the same portfolio, run by
the same manager:

- **Regular plan** — bought through a distributor, whose commission is paid out of the scheme's
  expense ratio.
- **Direct plan** — bought from the AMC directly or through a platform that does not earn
  commission, so no distribution cost is embedded.

The difference is typically 0.5% to 1.0% a year for equity schemes. Because the commission comes
out of the expense ratio, a direct plan's NAV grows slightly faster than the regular plan's for
the identical portfolio. There is no separate fee you avoid — the gap simply shows up in NAV.

## What 0.75% a year costs

On a ₹10,000 monthly SIP over 25 years at 12% gross, the direct plan's lower expense ratio leaves
materially more at the end — the gap runs into tens of lakhs, because the commission is charged
on the whole accumulated corpus every year, not on the amount you contribute.

The longer the horizon and larger the corpus, the more the difference matters. Over two or three
years it is nearly invisible; over twenty-five it is one of the largest controllable variables in
the outcome.

## When regular is the honest answer

Direct is cheaper, not automatically better. A distributor who stops a client from panic-selling
in a crash, keeps the SIP running, or prevents a portfolio of fifteen overlapping funds can
easily earn their 0.75% back. The adviser's value is behavioural, and for many investors that
value is real.

What matters is knowing which you are paying for. The problem case is paying distributor
commission indefinitely while receiving no advice.

## How to tell what you hold

Scheme names carry the label — "Direct" appears explicitly, and the plan is shown in your
consolidated account statement. A fund-house statement or the CAS from a depository will list it
per folio.

Switching a regular plan holding to direct is a **redemption and fresh purchase**, so it triggers
capital gains tax and restarts exit-load clocks. New contributions can be directed to the direct
plan without touching existing units.

**SEBI-registered investment advisers (RIAs)** charge a fee and cannot accept commissions, which
is the structural difference from distributors.
