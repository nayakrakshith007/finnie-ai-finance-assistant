## What Is a SIP?

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) is a method of investing a fixed amount into
a mutual fund at regular intervals — typically monthly. Each SIP installment
buys units of the fund at the current NAV (Net Asset Value).

SIP is not a type of mutual fund. It is a mode of investment, like how an EMI
is a mode of payment. The underlying product is a mutual fund.

---

## How SIP Works

You instruct your bank/AMC/broker to auto-debit a fixed amount on a fixed date
every month. That amount buys units at the prevailing NAV on that day.

**Example:**
- Monthly SIP: ₹5,000
- Month 1 NAV: ₹100 → 50 units bought
- Month 2 NAV: ₹80 → 62.5 units bought (market fell, more units for same money)
- Month 3 NAV: ₹110 → 45.5 units bought
- Average cost per unit over 3 months: ₹15,000 / 158 units = **₹94.9**
  (lower than the average NAV of ₹96.7)

This is **rupee cost averaging** — you automatically buy more units when prices
fall and fewer when they rise, without any timing decisions.

---

## The Power of Compounding via SIP

The longer the SIP runs, the more compounding amplifies returns.

**₹10,000/month SIP at 12% annual returns:**

| Duration | Total invested | Portfolio value | Gain |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 years | ₹12 lakh | ₹23.2 lakh | ₹11.2 lakh |
| 15 years | ₹18 lakh | ₹50.1 lakh | ₹32.1 lakh |
| 20 years | ₹24 lakh | ₹99.9 lakh | ₹75.9 lakh |
| 25 years | ₹30 lakh | ₹1.89 crore | ₹1.59 crore |
| 30 years | ₹36 lakh | ₹3.53 crore | ₹3.17 crore |

The last 10 years grow the portfolio far more than the first 20 — this is the
J-curve effect of compounding. Starting early is the single most powerful
factor.

## Step-Up SIP (Top-Up SIP)

A step-up SIP increases your investment amount by a fixed percentage or amount
each year, in line with salary growth.

**Example:**
- Starting SIP: ₹5,000/month
- Annual step-up: 10%
- Year 1: ₹5,000/month
- Year 2: ₹5,500/month
- Year 3: ₹6,050/month...

**₹5,000/month with 10% annual step-up vs flat ₹5,000/month over 20 years:**
- Flat SIP: ~₹49.9 lakh
- Step-up SIP: **~₹88.3 lakh** (77% more wealth)

Most AMCs allow you to set up a step-up SIP at the time of registration.

## How to Choose the Right Fund for SIP

**For long-term goals (7+ years):**
- **Large-cap index fund** (Nifty 50 or Sensex): Low cost, broad diversification
- **Flexi-cap fund**: Diversified across market caps
- **Mid-cap fund**: Higher return potential, higher volatility (allocate a portion)

**For medium-term goals (3–7 years):**
- **Multi-asset allocation fund**: Equity + debt + gold
- **Balanced advantage fund (BAF)**: Dynamically adjusts equity allocation

**For short-term goals (1–3 years):**
- SIP in equity funds is risky for short tenures; use debt funds or RD instead

**Key metrics to check:**
- Expense ratio: Below 0.5% for direct plans, below 1% for regular
- Consistency over 5–10 year periods, not just 1-year returns
- Fund manager track record

## Direct Plan vs Regular Plan

| Feature | Direct Plan | Regular Plan |
|---|---|---|
| Bought through | AMC website, MFD, apps | Bank, broker, MFD (intermediary) |
| Expense ratio | Lower by 0.5–1% | Higher (includes distributor commission) |
| Advice included | None | Financial intermediary may advise |
| Return difference | Higher by 0.5–1%/year | Lower |

**Over 20 years, 1% difference in expense ratio can cost 20–25% of final corpus.**
If you are comfortable choosing your own funds, direct plans are strongly
preferable. Apps like Zerodha Coin, Groww, Paytm Money offer direct plans.

## SIP Myths Debunked

**"I should pause SIP when markets fall."**
Wrong. Market falls are when SIP buys more units cheaply. Pausing destroys
the rupee cost averaging benefit. Stay invested.

**"I need to find the best day of the month to SIP."**
No research supports meaningful returns difference from the SIP date. Any date
works. Consistency matters far more.

**"SIP is risk-free because I invest systematically."**
SIP reduces timing risk through averaging, but equity SIPs still carry market
risk. If markets fall 40% and stay there, your portfolio loses value. Equity
is for long-term goals.

**"Higher past returns = better fund to SIP in."**
Past 1-year top performers frequently underperform in subsequent years. Use
3–5 year consistency and risk-adjusted returns instead.

## SIP vs Lump Sum

| Situation | Preferred approach |
|---|---|
| Regular monthly income | SIP |
| Received a windfall (bonus, inheritance) | Lump sum or STP over 6–12 months |
| Markets near all-time highs | SIP (removes timing pressure) |
| Markets down 20–30% | Lump sum or increase SIP amount |

A Systematic Transfer Plan (STP) lets you park a lump sum in a liquid fund and
auto-transfer a fixed amount monthly to an equity fund — combining the safety
of a liquid fund with the averaging benefit of SIP.

---

## Tax on SIP Returns

Each SIP installment is treated as a separate purchase for capital gains:
- Units held more than 1 year: **LTCG at 12.5%** on gains above ₹1.25 lakh/year
- Units held less than 1 year: **STCG at 20%**

When you redeem, the oldest units (FIFO) are sold first. If you've been SIPing
for 5+ years, most redeemed units qualify for LTCG rates.

---

## Summary

- SIP automates regular investment and benefits from rupee cost averaging.
- Start early — compounding over 20–30 years creates dramatic wealth.
- Use step-up SIP to grow contributions with income, multiplying long-term gains.
- Choose direct plans to save 0.5–1% per year in expense ratio.
- Never pause SIP during market downturns — that's exactly when you buy cheaply.
- SIP in equity for 7+ year goals; use debt/RD for shorter horizons.
