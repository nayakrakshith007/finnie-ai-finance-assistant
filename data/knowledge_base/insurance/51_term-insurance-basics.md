## What term insurance is

Term insurance is the simplest form of life cover. You pay a yearly premium, and if you die
during the policy term, the insurer pays your nominee a fixed amount called the sum assured.
If you survive the term, nothing is paid out. That "nothing back" feature is exactly why it is
cheap: the entire premium buys protection, with no savings component bundled in.

A 30-year-old non-smoker in India can typically buy ₹1 crore of cover for roughly ₹10,000 to
₹15,000 a year. The same ₹1 crore through an endowment or money-back policy would cost many
times more, because part of that premium is being diverted into a low-return savings pot.

## How much cover, and for how long

A widely used rule of thumb is 10 to 15 times your annual income, adjusted for two things:
outstanding loans, which your family would have to service, and dependants' future costs such
as a child's education. Someone earning ₹12 lakh a year with a ₹40 lakh home loan would be
looking at roughly ₹1.6 crore of cover rather than ₹1.2 crore.

The term should run until your dependants are financially independent — commonly to age 60,
when earned income stops anyway. Cover extending to 80 or 99 sounds thorough but costs
considerably more for years when nobody depends on your salary.

## What beginners get wrong

Three mistakes recur. First, buying cover through an employer alone: group cover ends when the
job ends, usually at the worst possible moment. Second, under-declaring health conditions or
tobacco use to lower the premium — this is the most common reason claims are rejected, and the
saving is small compared with the risk of a repudiated claim. Third, treating insurance as an
investment: a term policy's job is to replace lost income, not to grow wealth.

Premiums are locked at the age you buy, so the same cover costs more every year you wait.
