## Why Health Insurance Is Essential

A single hospitalisation in India can cost ₹2–10 lakh for serious conditions.
Medical inflation in India runs at 10–15% per year, far above general inflation.
Without health insurance, a medical emergency can wipe out years of savings.

Employer-provided group health insurance is a start but is not sufficient:
- It lapses when you leave the job
- The cover is often low (₹2–3 lakh)
- It may not cover parents

You need an independent, portable health policy that you own.

---

## Types of Health Insurance Plans

**Individual plan:** Covers one person. The sum insured applies to that one person.

**Family floater plan:** One sum insured shared by all family members. If the
cover is ₹10 lakh, any member can use up to ₹10 lakh in a year — but the full
amount cannot be used by two members simultaneously.

**Super top-up plan:** Activates after your base policy or out-of-pocket
expenses cross a deductible threshold. Very cost-effective way to increase total
cover without paying high premiums for a large base policy.

**Example:**
- Base policy: ₹5 lakh
- Super top-up: ₹20 lakh with ₹5 lakh deductible
- Total effective cover: ₹25 lakh
- Annual premium: ₹8,000–12,000 (much cheaper than a ₹25 lakh base policy)

---

## How Much Cover Is Enough?

As a rough guideline for Indian cities (2024):

| City tier | Minimum recommended cover |
|---|---|
| Metro (Mumbai, Delhi, Bengaluru) | ₹10–15 lakh per person |
| Tier-1 city | ₹7–10 lakh per person |
| Tier-2/3 city | ₹5–7 lakh per person |

Review and increase your cover every 3–5 years to account for medical inflation.

## Key Terms

**Sum insured:** Maximum amount the insurer pays per policy year.

**Premium:** Annual amount you pay to keep the policy active.

**Waiting period:**
- **Initial waiting period:** 30 days from policy start (no claims except for accidents).
- **Pre-existing disease (PED) waiting period:** Illnesses you had before buying
  the policy are typically not covered for 2–4 years. Disclosed at purchase.
- **Specific disease waiting period:** Some conditions (cataract, hernia, joint
  replacement) have 1–2 year waiting periods even for new policies.

**Copay / Co-payment:** Some policies require you to pay a fixed percentage of
every claim (e.g. 10% copay means you pay 10%, insurer pays 90%). Avoid policies
with high copay for primary coverage.

**Deductible:** A fixed amount you bear before the insurer pays. Similar to copay
but a flat sum rather than a percentage.

**Cashless claim:** You get treated at a network hospital and the insurer pays the
hospital directly. You pay only non-covered items.

**Reimbursement claim:** You pay first, then submit bills to the insurer for
reimbursement. Slower but needed at non-network hospitals.

**No-claim bonus (NCB):** Every claim-free year increases your sum insured by
5–10% at no extra cost. Some policies can double your cover this way over time.

## What to Look for When Choosing a Policy

1. **Room rent limit:** Avoid policies that cap room rent at 1% of sum insured —
   this causes proportionate deductions across your entire bill.
2. **Restoration benefit:** Replenishes your sum insured within the same year if
   it is exhausted. Essential for floater policies with multiple members.
3. **Network hospitals:** Check whether your preferred hospitals are in the
   insurer's cashless network.
4. **Sub-limits:** Some older policies cap specific costs (ICU, specific surgeries).
   Prefer policies without sub-limits.
5. **Day-care procedures:** Modern policies cover procedures that don't require
   24-hour hospitalisation (e.g. chemotherapy, dialysis). Confirm this is included.

## Senior Citizens and Parents

Standard health policies become very expensive above age 60.

Options for parents:
- **Senior citizen plans:** Specifically designed for 60+ with higher premiums
  but higher acceptance
- **Super top-up for parents:** Add a super top-up over their existing employer/
  government policy (CGHS, ESI) if applicable
- **Buy early:** Add parents to a floater or buy them a plan before they develop
  pre-existing conditions

## Tax Benefit — Section 80D

Premiums paid for health insurance are deductible under Section 80D:

| Who is covered | Deduction limit |
|---|---|
| Self + family (below 60) | ₹25,000/year |
| Self + family + parents (parents below 60) | ₹25,000 + ₹25,000 = ₹50,000 |
| Self + family + senior citizen parents | ₹25,000 + ₹50,000 = ₹75,000 |
| Self (senior citizen) + family | ₹50,000 |

Preventive health check-up expenses (up to ₹5,000) count within these limits.

---

## Summary

- Get health insurance independently — do not rely only on employer cover.
- Cover amount: ₹10–15 lakh for metro cities; use a super top-up to extend it
  cheaply.
- Check room rent limits, sub-limits, and restoration benefit before buying.
- Add parents early, before pre-existing conditions develop.
- Premiums are deductible under 80D — maximum ₹75,000 for self + senior parents.
