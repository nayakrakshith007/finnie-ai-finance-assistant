## Claim settlement ratio, and its limits

The claim settlement ratio (CSR) is the share of claims an insurer settled out of those it
received in a year. IRDAI publishes it annually. A life insurer settling 98% of claims looks
better than one settling 92%, and for life insurance the measure is reasonably meaningful because
claims are binary: a death claim is paid or it is not.

The number is weaker than it looks, though. It counts claims, not rupees, so an insurer can post
a high ratio while rejecting a few very large claims. It also says nothing about *how long*
settlement took.

## The metrics that matter alongside it

- **Amount settlement ratio** — the share of claimed *value* paid, which catches the problem
  above.
- **Claim repudiation rate** — outright rejections, as distinct from claims still pending.
- **Average settlement time** — a claim paid in two weeks is materially different from one paid
  in six months, particularly for a family that has lost its earner.
- **Grievance count per 10,000 policies** — IRDAI publishes complaint data; persistently high
  complaint volumes signal servicing problems that no ratio captures.
- **Solvency ratio** — IRDAI requires a minimum of 1.50. It measures whether the insurer can meet
  its obligations at all.

## For health insurance specifically

CSR matters less than in life insurance, because health claims are frequently settled
*partially* — the claim counts as settled even if the insurer paid 60% of the bill after applying
room-rent proportionality or sub-limits. For health cover, the incidence of claims paid in full,
the network hospital list, and whether claims are processed in-house or by a third-party
administrator tend to be more informative.

## Where to find the data

IRDAI's annual report carries insurer-wise claim and grievance statistics, and the regulator's
policyholder portal hosts consumer-facing summaries. Insurer websites also publish their own
public disclosures quarterly.
