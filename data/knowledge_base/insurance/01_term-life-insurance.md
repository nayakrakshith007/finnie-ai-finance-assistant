## What Is Term Life Insurance?

Term life insurance is the simplest and most affordable form of life insurance.
You pay a fixed premium every year for a chosen term (e.g. 30 years). If you
die during that term, your nominee receives the sum assured (the cover amount).
If you survive the term, the policy expires and you receive nothing — there is
no maturity benefit.

This is fundamentally different from endowment plans or ULIPs, which combine
insurance with investment. Term plans are pure protection.

---

## Why Term Insurance Is Non-Negotiable

If anyone financially depends on your income — a spouse, children, parents —
and you were to die suddenly, their financial life would be disrupted. Term
insurance replaces your income for your dependants.

**Rule of thumb for cover amount:** 10–15× your annual income, or enough to
replace your income until your youngest dependant is financially independent.

**Example:**
- Annual income: ₹10 lakh
- Dependants need 25 years of support
- Recommended cover: ₹1–1.5 crore

---

## How Much Does It Cost?

Term insurance is very affordable for young, healthy people.

| Age at purchase | Annual premium (₹1 crore cover, 30-year term) |
|---|---|
| 25 years | ₹7,000 – ₹10,000/year |
| 30 years | ₹10,000 – ₹14,000/year |
| 35 years | ₹14,000 – ₹20,000/year |
| 40 years | ₹22,000 – ₹32,000/year |

Premiums rise sharply with age. Buy early.

---

## Key Terms You Must Know

**Sum assured:** The amount your nominee receives on your death. Choose this
based on your income replacement need, not the premium size.

**Policy term:** How long the cover lasts. Cover until age 60–65 (your likely
retirement age) is a common recommendation.

**Premium payment term:** You can pay regularly throughout the policy term
(regular pay), for a limited period (limited pay), or as a one-time lump sum
(single pay). Regular pay is simplest.

**Nominee:** The person who receives the claim. Keep this updated — an outdated
nominee (e.g. a deceased parent) causes claim delays.

**Waiting period / suicide clause:** Most policies exclude claims in the first
year for suicide. All other causes of death from day 1 are covered.

---

## Term vs Endowment vs ULIP

| Feature | Term | Endowment | ULIP |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pure protection | Yes | No | No |
| Maturity benefit | None | Yes | Yes |
| Premium for ₹1 cr cover | ₹8,000–14,000/yr | ₹5–10 lakh/yr | ₹1–3 lakh/yr |
| Recommended | Yes | Generally no | Generally no |
| IRR if you survive | 0% | 4–5% | Varies |

Endowment and ULIP policies give a maturity benefit but charge enormously more
for the same cover. Buy term insurance for protection and invest separately for
wealth creation — do not mix the two.

---

## Riders Worth Considering

**Critical illness rider:** Pays a lump sum on diagnosis of specified illnesses
(cancer, heart attack, stroke). Good addition if you don't have separate health
insurance for large medical costs.

**Accidental death benefit rider:** Pays an additional sum if death is due to
an accident. Usually cheap — worth adding.

**Waiver of premium rider:** Waives future premiums if you become permanently
disabled. Useful if you are the sole earner.

---

## How to Choose a Term Insurer

**Claim settlement ratio (CSR):** Percentage of death claims settled. Look for
CSR above 95%. Published annually by IRDAI.

**Solvency ratio:** Financial health of the insurer. Must be above 1.5 (IRDAI
minimum). Higher is safer.

**Avoid churning:** Once you have a policy, do not cancel it to switch. Your
age and possibly your health will be re-evaluated at a higher premium.

---

## Tax Benefit

Premiums paid are deductible under Section 80C (up to ₹1.5 lakh/year).
The death benefit received by the nominee is fully tax-free under Section 10(10D).

---

## Summary

- Buy term insurance if anyone depends on your income.
- Cover = 10–15× annual income, term until retirement age.
- Buy young — premiums double or triple between 25 and 40.
- Do not mix insurance with investment — separate them.
- Check CSR before choosing an insurer.
