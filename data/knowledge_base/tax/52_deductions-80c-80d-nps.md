## These belong to the old regime

Almost every deduction below is available **only if you opt for the old tax regime**. The new
regime offers lower slab rates but removes most deductions. Which works out better depends on
how much you would actually claim — covered separately in the regime-comparison article.

## Section 80C — up to ₹1.5 lakh

A single combined ceiling of ₹1.5 lakh across a long list of instruments and expenses:

- EPF and VPF (employee's own contribution)
- PPF, NSC, Sukanya Samriddhi, five-year post office deposits
- ELSS mutual funds (three-year lock-in, the shortest among 80C options)
- Life insurance premiums, for self, spouse, children
- Principal repayment on a home loan
- Tuition fees for up to two children
- Five-year tax-saving bank fixed deposits

Most salaried people are already partway there through EPF and term insurance premiums before
making any dedicated investment — a point worth checking before buying a product purely for the
deduction.

## Section 80CCD — the NPS additions

- **80CCD(1)** — your own NPS contribution, but it shares the ₹1.5 lakh 80C ceiling.
- **80CCD(1B)** — an **additional ₹50,000** for NPS Tier-I, over and above 80C. This is the only
  way to push total deductions past ₹2 lakh through investment alone.
- **80CCD(2)** — the *employer's* NPS contribution, up to 10% of salary (14% for government
  employees). This sits outside the ₹1.5 lakh limit and, notably, **remains available under the
  new regime**, which makes it one of the few live deductions there.

## Section 80D — health insurance

| Premium paid for | Deduction |
|---|---|
| Self, spouse, children (below 60) | ₹25,000 |
| Self and family, where the eldest insured is 60+ | ₹50,000 |
| Parents (below 60) | additional ₹25,000 |
| Parents (60+) | additional ₹50,000 |

Preventive health check-ups count within these limits up to ₹5,000 and may be paid in cash;
premiums themselves must be paid by a non-cash mode to qualify.

## Others worth knowing

**80E** — interest on an education loan, no cap, for eight years. **80TTA** — ₹10,000 on savings
account interest. **80TTB** — ₹50,000 on deposit interest for senior citizens. **24(b)** —
₹2 lakh on home loan interest for a self-occupied property, separate from the 80C principal
deduction.

*Educational information only. Limits and regime rules change with each Finance Act — verify
current provisions on the Income Tax Department portal or with a qualified tax professional.*
