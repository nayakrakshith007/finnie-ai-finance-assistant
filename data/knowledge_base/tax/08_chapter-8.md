# IMPORTANT: Capital Gains Tax Rates Have Changed

> **Earlier chapters in this module were written before Finance Act 2018. The
> tax rates in those chapters are outdated. This chapter supersedes them for
> capital gains on equity and equity mutual funds.**

---

## 8.1 — History of LTCG on Equity

Before 1 February 2018, long-term capital gains (LTCG) on listed equity shares
and equity-oriented mutual funds were **completely exempt from tax** (0%). That
exemption was removed by Finance Act 2018.

---

## 8.2 — Current LTCG Rates (as of Budget 2024, effective 23 July 2024)

| Asset type | Holding period | LTCG rate | Exemption limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Listed equity shares / equity MF | > 1 year | **12.5%** | First ₹1.25 lakh of gains per FY is exempt |
| Debt mutual funds (bought after 1 Apr 2023) | Any | Taxed as income (slab rate) | No LTCG benefit |
| Debt mutual funds (bought before 1 Apr 2023) | > 3 years | 12.5% without indexation | — |
| Real estate | > 2 years | 12.5% without indexation (Budget 2024 removed indexation) | — |
| Gold | > 3 years | 12.5% without indexation | — |
| Non-listed shares | > 2 years | 12.5% | — |

**Key point:** LTCG on equity is NOT exempt. Gains above ₹1.25 lakh per
financial year are taxed at **12.5%** (no indexation benefit).

---

## 8.3 — Current STCG Rates (as of Budget 2024)

| Asset type | Holding period | STCG rate |
|---|---|---|
| Listed equity shares / equity MF | ≤ 1 year | **20%** (was 15% before 23 Jul 2024) |
| Debt MF / bonds | Any | Slab rate |

---

## 8.4 — The ₹1.25 Lakh Exemption: How It Works

The first ₹1.25 lakh of LTCG from equity/equity MF in a financial year is
exempt. Only gains **above** that threshold are taxed at 12.5%.

**Example:**
- You sell equity shares after holding for 2 years
- Total LTCG = ₹2,00,000
- Exempt amount = ₹1,25,000
- Taxable LTCG = ₹75,000
- Tax = 12.5% × ₹75,000 = **₹9,375**

---

## 8.5 — Tax-Loss Harvesting (Legal Way to Reduce LTCG)

One legitimate strategy to reduce LTCG tax:

**Tax-loss harvesting** means selling loss-making equity investments before
year-end to offset gains. Long-term capital losses can be set off against
long-term capital gains.

Steps:
1. Identify investments in your portfolio that are at a loss (unrealised).
2. Sell them before 31 March to realise the loss.
3. The realised long-term capital loss offsets your LTCG for the year.
4. You can carry forward unused LTCG losses for up to 8 assessment years.

> ⚠️ Note: Short-term capital losses can be set off against both STCG and LTCG.
> But long-term capital losses can only be set off against LTCG, not STCG.

---

## 8.6 — What Changed in Budget 2024 vs Budget 2018

| Parameter | Before Budget 2018 | Budget 2018 to 22 Jul 2024 | Budget 2024 (from 23 Jul 2024) |
|---|---|---|---|
| LTCG on equity | 0% (fully exempt) | 10% above ₹1 lakh | **12.5%** above ₹1.25 lakh |
| STCG on equity | 15% | 15% | **20%** |
| LTCG exemption limit | Full exemption | ₹1 lakh | ₹1.25 lakh |

---

## 8.7 — Summary

- LTCG on equity/equity MF: **12.5%** on gains exceeding ₹1.25 lakh per year.
- STCG on equity: **20%**.
- Equity LTCG is **not exempt** — the old 0% rate no longer exists.
- Tax-loss harvesting is the primary legal way to reduce LTCG tax.
- There are no "loopholes" to avoid LTCG; the exemption limit and loss set-off
  are the only legitimate reduction mechanisms.
