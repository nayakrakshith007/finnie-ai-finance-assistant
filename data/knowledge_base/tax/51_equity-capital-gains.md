## Short-term versus long-term

For **listed equity shares and equity-oriented mutual funds**, the dividing line is a holding
period of 12 months:

- Held **12 months or less** — short-term capital gain (STCG), taxed at **20%**.
- Held **more than 12 months** — long-term capital gain (LTCG), taxed at **12.5%** on gains
  **above ₹1.25 lakh** in a financial year.

These rates took effect for transfers on or after 23 July 2024, replacing the earlier 15% STCG
and 10% LTCG with a ₹1 lakh exemption. Surcharge and cess apply on top.

The ₹1.25 lakh exemption is an annual aggregate across all equity LTCG, not per scheme or per
transaction.

## A worked example

Suppose you invested ₹5 lakh in an equity fund, sold after two years for ₹7 lakh:

| | |
|---|---|
| Gain | ₹2,00,000 |
| Exempt | ₹1,25,000 |
| Taxable | ₹75,000 |
| Tax at 12.5% | ₹9,375 |

Had the same gain been realised within 12 months, the whole ₹2 lakh would be taxed at 20% —
₹40,000.

## Other asset classes differ

- **Debt mutual funds** bought on or after 1 April 2023: gains taxed at your **slab rate**
  regardless of holding period, with no indexation.
- **Unlisted shares and immovable property**: 24 months for long-term, taxed at 12.5% without
  indexation, with a limited grandfathering option for property acquired before 23 July 2024.
- **Gold ETFs, international funds, hybrid funds**: treatment depends on the fund's equity
  allocation, so category matters.

## Set-off and carry-forward

Capital losses can be set off against capital gains: short-term losses against either STCG or
LTCG, long-term losses only against LTCG. Unabsorbed losses carry forward **eight assessment
years**, but only if you file your return by the due date — a frequently missed condition.

**Grandfathering:** for equity bought before 1 February 2018, the cost is treated as the higher
of actual cost and the 31 January 2018 fair market value.

*Educational information only. Rates and thresholds change with each Finance Act — verify current
provisions on the Income Tax Department portal or with a qualified tax professional before
filing.*
