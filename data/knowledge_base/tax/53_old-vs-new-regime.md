## Two systems, one choice each year

Since the new regime became the default, a taxpayer must decide which to be assessed under. The
trade is simple to state: the **new regime** has lower slab rates but strips out most
deductions; the **old regime** keeps deductions but taxes at higher rates.

Salaried taxpayers may switch between regimes year to year. Taxpayers with business or
professional income face a restriction: having opted out of the new regime, they can return to
it only once.

## What survives in the new regime

Most deductions do not, but these do:

- **Standard deduction** for salaried taxpayers and pensioners
- **80CCD(2)** — employer's NPS contribution
- Employer's EPF contribution
- Certain allowances such as transport allowance for specially-abled employees

What is lost: 80C entirely, 80D, 24(b) home loan interest on a self-occupied property, HRA
exemption, LTA, 80E, 80TTA.

## How to actually decide

The comparison is arithmetic, and the honest way to run it is both calculations side by side on
your own numbers:

1. Total the deductions you would **genuinely** claim under the old regime — not the theoretical
   maximum. EPF, term insurance premium, health insurance, home loan interest, HRA.
2. Compute tax under the old regime on income after those deductions.
3. Compute tax under the new regime on income after only the standard deduction.
4. Pick the lower number.

The pattern that emerges: taxpayers with **large genuine deductions** — typically a home loan
running alongside HRA, high insurance premiums, and a maxed 80C — often still come out ahead
under the old regime. Those **without a home loan and with modest deductions** usually pay less
under the new regime, which is why it was made the default.

## Where people go wrong

Two errors recur. The first is buying an investment product chiefly for its deduction while
sitting under the new regime, where the deduction does nothing. The second is assuming last
year's choice still holds — slab rates, the rebate threshold, and the standard deduction have all
been revised in recent Budgets, so the better regime can flip from one year to the next.

The Income Tax Department hosts an official comparison calculator on its portal, which is more
reliable than any third-party version because it is updated when provisions change.

*Educational information only. Slab rates, rebate limits and the standard deduction change with
each Finance Act — run the current year's numbers on the Income Tax Department portal or with a
qualified tax professional.*
