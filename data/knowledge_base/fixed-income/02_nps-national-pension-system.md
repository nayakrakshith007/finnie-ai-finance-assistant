## What Is the NPS?

The National Pension System (NPS) is a voluntary, long-term retirement savings
scheme regulated by the Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority
(PFRDA). It is a market-linked pension product — your contributions are invested
in a portfolio of equity, government bonds, and corporate bonds, and returns
depend on market performance.

**Who can invest:** Indian citizens aged 18–70 (OCI holders also eligible).
Government employees have a mandatory NPS; private sector employees and
self-employed individuals can join voluntarily.

---

## Account Types

**Tier 1 (Pension account):**
- Primary retirement account
- Mandatory for all NPS subscribers
- Withdrawals restricted before age 60; exceptions for specific reasons
- Minimum contribution: ₹500 per contribution; ₹1,000 per year
- All tax benefits apply to Tier 1

**Tier 2 (Savings account):**
- Optional; like a voluntary savings account linked to NPS
- No withdrawal restrictions — can withdraw anytime
- No tax benefits under 80C (except for central government employees)
- Minimum contribution: ₹250 per contribution

---

## Investment Choices

NPS offers two modes of investment:

**Active choice:** You decide the asset allocation across:
- **Equity (E):** Up to 75% (capped at 50% above age 50, reducing by 2.5%/year)
  — invests in equity index funds
- **Corporate bonds (C):** Up to 100%
- **Government bonds (G):** Up to 100%
- **Alternative assets (A):** Up to 5%

**Auto choice (Lifecycle fund):** Allocation automatically shifts from equity to
bonds as you age:
- Aggressive: Higher equity at younger ages (up to 75%)
- Moderate: Balanced approach
- Conservative: Lower equity throughout

For most long-term investors under 45, a higher equity allocation typically
produces better retirement outcomes.

## Tax Benefits — The Key Differentiator

NPS has the most generous tax deduction structure of any Indian investment:

| Section | Benefit | Who qualifies |
|---|---|---|
| 80CCD(1) | Up to ₹1.5 lakh/year | All subscribers (counts within 80C limit) |
| 80CCD(1B) | Additional ₹50,000/year | All subscribers (over and above 80C) |
| 80CCD(2) | Up to 10% of salary (14% for central govt) | Employer contribution; not from salary |

**Combined maximum for a salaried individual:**
- 80C: ₹1.5 lakh (NPS contribution included)
- 80CCD(1B): ₹50,000 extra
- **Total NPS-related deduction: up to ₹2 lakh/year**

**Employer NPS contribution:** Under 80CCD(2), employer contributions to your
NPS account are deductible beyond the ₹1.5 lakh 80C ceiling. This is the most
tax-efficient perk an employer can offer — ₹1 lakh employer NPS contribution
saves ₹30,000 in tax (30% bracket).

---

## Withdrawals and Exit Rules

**On retirement (at 60):**
- Minimum 40% of corpus must be used to purchase an annuity (pension)
- Maximum 60% can be withdrawn as a lump sum (tax-free)
- Annuity income is taxable at your slab rate

**Before 60 (partial withdrawal):**
- Allowed after 3 years of account opening
- Maximum 25% of own contributions (not returns)
- For specified reasons only: higher education, marriage, house purchase, illness,
  disability, or starting a business

**Premature exit (before 60):**
- Minimum 80% must go to annuity; only 20% can be withdrawn as lump sum
- Higher annuity requirement makes early exit costly

**At death:** Nominee receives 100% of corpus; no annuity requirement.

## NPS Returns — What to Expect

NPS returns are market-linked and vary by asset allocation and fund manager.

**Historical annualised returns (approximate, as of 2024):**

| Asset class | 10-year return |
|---|---|
| Equity (E) | 12–14% |
| Corporate bonds (C) | 7–9% |
| Government bonds (G) | 7–8% |

A typical Tier 1 account with 60% equity, 30% corporate bonds, 10% govt bonds
might yield **10–12% annualised** over a long horizon, net of fund management
charges (0.01–0.09% per year — among the lowest in the world).

**Comparison to PPF:**
- NPS (equity-heavy) can grow at 2–4× the rate of PPF over 20–30 years
- PPF is guaranteed; NPS has market risk
- NPS tax benefit (₹2 lakh) is larger than PPF (₹1.5 lakh within 80C)

## How to Open an NPS Account

**Online (eNPS):** Visit enps.nsdl.com or enps.karvy.com. Requires Aadhaar OTP
or PAN + bank details. Completed digitally in 15–20 minutes.

**Offline:** Through Points of Presence (PoP) — most banks (SBI, HDFC, ICICI)
are registered PoPs. Submit physical KYC forms.

You receive a **Permanent Retirement Account Number (PRAN)** — this is your
NPS identity, portable across jobs and employers.

## NPS vs PPF vs ELSS

| Feature | NPS (equity) | PPF | ELSS |
|---|---|---|---|
| Returns | 10–13% (market) | 7.1% (guaranteed) | 12–14% (market) |
| Tax deduction | ₹2 lakh (80C + 80CCD1B) | ₹1.5 lakh (80C) | ₹1.5 lakh (80C) |
| Lock-in | Until 60 | 15 years | 3 years |
| Tax on maturity | 60% tax-free; 40% annuity | Fully tax-free | LTCG (12.5% above ₹1.25L) |
| Annuity requirement | 40% of corpus | None | None |

**Best use of NPS:** Primarily for the extra ₹50,000 Section 80CCD(1B)
deduction that no other instrument offers. After fully using 80C (PPF, ELSS,
home loan etc.), NPS Tier 1 saves ₹10,000–15,000 in tax per year (30% bracket).

---

## Summary

- NPS is a government-regulated pension scheme with market-linked returns.
- Unique Section 80CCD(1B) gives ₹50,000 extra deduction beyond 80C's ₹1.5 lakh.
- Choose high equity allocation in Tier 1 when young — it's cheap (0.09% fees)
  and historically outperforms other fixed-income alternatives.
- Partial withdrawal is allowed for education, house, illness — with limits.
- At maturity, 40% goes to annuity; 60% is a tax-free lump sum.
- Best strategy: use NPS primarily for the extra tax deduction; invest the rest
  in ELSS/MFs for greater flexibility.
