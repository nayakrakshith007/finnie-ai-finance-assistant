## Overview of Fixed-Income Instruments

Fixed-income instruments are investments where the return is predetermined or
linked to a benchmark rate — you know roughly what you'll earn. They carry
lower risk than equity but typically offer lower long-term returns.

In India, the main fixed-income options for retail investors are:

1. Fixed Deposit (FD)
2. Recurring Deposit (RD)
3. Public Provident Fund (PPF)
4. National Savings Certificate (NSC)
5. Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY)

---

## 1. Fixed Deposit (FD)

**What it is:** You deposit a lump sum with a bank or post office for a fixed
term (7 days to 10 years) and earn a predetermined interest rate.

**How returns work:** Interest is paid periodically (monthly, quarterly,
annually) or reinvested and paid at maturity (cumulative FD). Most banks offer
~0.25–0.50% higher rates to senior citizens.

**Typical rates (2024–25):** 6.5–7.5% per year for 1–3 year FDs at major banks;
small finance banks offer 7.5–9.0%.

**Tax treatment:**
- Interest earned is fully taxable as income at your slab rate
- TDS deducted at 10% if annual FD interest exceeds ₹40,000 (₹50,000 for senior
  citizens); submit Form 15G/15H if income is below taxable threshold
- No 80C benefit for regular FDs
- **Tax-saver FD (5-year lock-in):** Qualifies for 80C deduction up to ₹1.5 lakh;
  premature withdrawal not allowed

**Premature withdrawal:** Allowed in most cases with a 0.5–1% penalty on the
applicable rate. Some FDs allow partial withdrawal.

**Safety:** Deposits at scheduled commercial banks are insured up to ₹5 lakh
per depositor per bank under the DICGC. Post office FDs are backed by the
government and have no insurance cap.

---

## 2. Recurring Deposit (RD)

**What it is:** A monthly savings scheme where you invest a fixed amount every
month and earn FD-like interest rates.

**Use case:** Disciplined savings towards a goal (vacation, appliance, school
fees) over 6 months to 10 years.

**Rates:** Similar to FDs, typically 6.5–7.5% at major banks.

**Tax treatment:** Interest is taxable at slab rate; TDS applies if aggregate
interest exceeds the same threshold as FDs. No 80C benefit.

---

## 3. Public Provident Fund (PPF)

**What it is:** A long-term government savings scheme with a 15-year maturity,
extendable in 5-year blocks.

**Who it's for:** Anyone who wants guaranteed, government-backed, tax-free
returns over a long horizon.

**Key features:**
- **Interest rate:** Set quarterly by the government; currently **7.1% per year**
  (compounded annually)
- **Investment limits:** ₹500 minimum to ₹1.5 lakh maximum per financial year
- **Lock-in:** 15 years; partial withdrawals allowed from year 7; premature
  closure allowed from year 6 for specific reasons (medical, education)
- **Loan against PPF:** Allowed from year 3 to year 6 against the balance

**Tax treatment (EEE — triple tax benefit):**
- Contributions up to ₹1.5 lakh/year deductible under Section 80C
- Interest earned is completely tax-free
- Maturity amount is fully tax-free

**Returns example:**
- Monthly investment: ₹5,000 (₹60,000/year)
- Over 15 years at 7.1%: **~₹16.3 lakh** (invested ₹9 lakh, earned ₹7.3 lakh tax-free)

**Where to open:** Any nationalised bank or post office branch; also available
online via net banking at SBI, PNB, HDFC Bank, etc.

---

## 4. National Savings Certificate (NSC)

**What it is:** A post office savings instrument with a fixed 5-year maturity
and guaranteed government-backed returns.

**Key features:**
- **Interest rate:** Currently **7.7% per year** (compounded annually but paid
  at maturity)
- **Minimum investment:** ₹1,000; no maximum
- **Lock-in:** 5 years; no premature withdrawal (except on death or court order)
- **Nomination:** Available

**Tax treatment:**
- Investment qualifies for Section 80C deduction (up to ₹1.5 lakh/year)
- Interest accrued each year is treated as reinvested — eligible for 80C deduction
  again each year (except the final year)
- Final maturity interest is taxable at slab rate
- Net tax benefit depends on your slab

**When NSC makes sense:**
- You are in a low tax bracket (5% or nil)
- You want 80C benefit beyond PPF's ₹1.5 lakh limit (NSC has no upper limit)
- You need a fixed 5-year instrument

---

## 5. Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY)

**What it is:** A government scheme for the financial security of a girl child.
Opened in the name of a girl child below age 10.

**Key features:**
- **Interest rate:** Currently **8.2% per year** (highest among all government
  small savings schemes)
- **Investment limits:** ₹250 minimum to ₹1.5 lakh maximum per financial year
- **Deposits required:** For the first 15 years from account opening
- **Maturity:** When the girl turns 21 (or at marriage after age 18)
- **Partial withdrawal:** 50% of balance allowed after the girl turns 18, for
  education or marriage expenses

**Tax treatment (EEE):**
- Contributions up to ₹1.5 lakh/year qualify under Section 80C
- Interest is fully tax-free
- Maturity amount is fully tax-free

**Who should use it:** Any parent of a girl child — the combination of the
highest government scheme rate + EEE tax treatment makes it exceptional for
long-term education and marriage funding.

## Comparison Table

| Instrument | Rate | Tax on returns | 80C | Lock-in | Backed by |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FD (regular) | 6.5–7.5% | Taxable | No | Flexible | Bank/DICGC |
| Tax-saver FD | 6.5–7.5% | Taxable | Yes | 5 years | Bank/DICGC |
| RD | 6.5–7.5% | Taxable | No | Flexible | Bank |
| PPF | 7.1% | Tax-free | Yes | 15 years | Government |
| NSC | 7.7% | Partly taxable | Yes | 5 years | Government |
| SSY | 8.2% | Tax-free | Yes | 21 years | Government |

---

## Summary

- FDs are flexible and liquid; interest is taxable; use tax-saver FD for 80C.
- RDs are for monthly savings towards short-term goals.
- PPF is excellent for long-term tax-free savings; EEE with 7.1% guaranteed.
- NSC gives 7.7% with 80C benefit; suitable for those in lower tax brackets.
- SSY is the best government scheme for a girl child — 8.2% EEE.
- All government schemes (PPF, NSC, SSY) are sovereign-backed, zero credit risk.
