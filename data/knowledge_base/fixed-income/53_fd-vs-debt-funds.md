## The core difference

A bank fixed deposit is a contract: you lend the bank money at an agreed rate for an agreed
term, and the rate does not change once booked. A debt mutual fund pools money and buys bonds,
treasury bills, or commercial paper, and its NAV moves daily as those holdings are marked to
market. The FD gives certainty; the debt fund gives liquidity and market-linked returns.

Deposits at scheduled commercial banks are insured by DICGC up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per
bank, covering principal and interest together. Debt funds carry no such guarantee — they carry
credit risk from the issuers they lend to and interest-rate risk from bond price movements.

## Taxation after April 2023

This is where the comparison changed. For debt mutual fund units purchased on or after
1 April 2023, gains are taxed at your income slab rate regardless of holding period — the
earlier long-term capital gains treatment with indexation no longer applies to them.

FD interest has always been taxed at slab rate, and banks deduct TDS once interest crosses the
annual threshold.

So on tax treatment the two are now broadly level. What remains different is *timing*: FD
interest is taxed as it accrues each year, while a debt fund is taxed only when you redeem.
Deferring the tax event still has value for a multi-year holding.

## Practical trade-offs

| | Fixed deposit | Debt mutual fund |
|---|---|---|
| Return certainty | Rate locked at booking | Market-linked, varies |
| Exit before term | Premature withdrawal penalty, typically 0.5–1% | Redeem at NAV; some funds levy a short exit load |
| Credit risk | DICGC cover to ₹5 lakh | Depends on portfolio quality |
| Minimum sensible horizon | Matches the chosen term | Varies by category: overnight and liquid funds for days to weeks, short-duration for one to three years |
| Taxed when | Interest accrues annually | Units are redeemed |

## Matching category to horizon

Debt fund categories differ enormously in risk. Overnight and liquid funds hold very
short-maturity paper and barely move; gilt and long-duration funds can swing several percent
when rates shift. Matching the fund's duration to the time until the money is needed is the main
risk control in debt investing.

*Educational information only. Tax rules change with each Finance Act — verify current treatment
with the Income Tax Department before acting.*
