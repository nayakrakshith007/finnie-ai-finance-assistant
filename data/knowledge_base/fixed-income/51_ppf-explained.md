## What PPF is

The Public Provident Fund is a government-backed savings scheme with a 15-year term. The
interest rate is set by the Ministry of Finance and revised quarterly — it has been 7.1% per
annum for several years. Interest is compounded annually and credited at year end.

Deposits range from ₹500 to ₹1.5 lakh per financial year, in up to 12 instalments. Missing the
₹500 minimum makes the account dormant, revivable with a small penalty plus arrears.

## The EEE tax treatment

PPF is one of the few Indian instruments with **exempt-exempt-exempt** status: the deposit
qualifies for deduction under Section 80C (old tax regime), the interest accrues tax-free, and
the maturity amount is tax-free. For someone in the 30% slab under the old regime, a tax-free
7.1% is roughly equivalent to a taxable 10.1%.

Note the regime dependency: the 80C deduction is unavailable under the new tax regime, though
the interest and maturity remain tax-free either way.

## Lock-in, partial withdrawal, and extension

The 15-year term is calculated from the end of the financial year in which the account was
opened, so it is effectively 15 years plus a few months. Partial withdrawal is allowed from year
seven, capped at 50% of the balance at the end of the fourth preceding year. Loans against the
balance are available between years three and six.

At maturity there are three options: withdraw everything, extend in five-year blocks with
further contributions, or extend without contributions while the balance keeps earning interest.

## Where it fits and where it does not

PPF suits the debt portion of a long-horizon goal, particularly retirement, because sovereign
backing removes credit risk and the tax treatment is hard to beat for a fixed-income product.
The constraints are real, though: the ₹1.5 lakh annual cap limits how much can be routed through
it, the 15-year lock-in makes it unsuitable for money needed sooner, and the rate is reset by
government rather than contracted for the full term.

*Educational information only. Rates are revised quarterly — verify the current rate and rules
with the National Savings Institute or your bank or post office.*
